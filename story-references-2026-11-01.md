# Additional story references - 1 November 2026

Reviewed 1 November 2026 by watching the reels through and sampling frames over time, so the motion notes below are first-hand observations of what actually moves on screen, not guesses from stills. The exception is the Faith vs Fact entry, which comes from a separate reviewer's dense frame-by-frame check and is marked as such. Audio was not heard on any reel, so nothing here claims anything about narration, music, voiceover or audio sync. No production tools were tested. No like counts are treated as proof of performance.

The focus of this set is the animation itself: how each reel moves, cuts and captions, and what that teaches for our own production.

## Aura Wonderworks - baby Krishna carried over the Yamuna

Account: https://www.instagram.com/aurawonderworks/reels/

Video: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfHvCwLTigq/

- 72 seconds, 16:9, labelled AI content
- Motion: a montage of AI-painted video clips joined by hard cuts roughly every 10-14 seconds - palace in rain, Vasudev carrying the basket with the serpent spread overhead, a lamp-lit interior, the child with the milk pot, the river lift, the Yashoda embrace
- Frame-pair check within one shot showed real in-scene subject motion (rain, water, cloth, breathing) concentrated on the characters, not a camera drifting over a still painting

What to learn: this is the full-animation tier - short generated video clips with genuine motion inside each shot, cut like a film montage. A painted storybook register can carry well-known Krishna scenes through feeling rather than spectacle, and 16:9 suits wide river and sky compositions

Source caution: the scenes are familiar Krishna lore, the specific narrative details have not been independently verified against a named source here

## Animation patterns across the set

Three workable motion tiers show up, cheapest last:

1. Full clip animation - generated video shots with real motion inside each one, cut as a montage (Aura Wonderworks)
2. Element and diagram animation - procedural builds, puppet-style pose cycles, effects like pulses and rays (Innertune, Faith vs Fact)
3. Stills carried by editing - static or near-static art where cuts, caption animation and one hero transformation supply the movement (Poe, Dead Lear, The Rishi Katha, most of The Hindu Archive)

Caption animation is load-bearing in almost every reel: word-by-word highlights (Innertune), one bold word (The Hindu Archive), colour-accent word swaps (Poe), word-by-word serif fades with gold keywords (The Rishi Katha), small-caps phrase builds (Faith vs Fact)

Cut cadence ranges from about 4-5 seconds (Faith vs Fact) to 10-15 seconds (Aura Wonderworks, The Rishi Katha). Cameras are mostly static - the motion comes from inside the frame or from the cut, not from pans and zooms

A single late animated beat - Hanuman growing, the rainbow payoff - can serve as the emotional peak of a reel that is otherwise mostly stills

## How these fit the collection

The painted Krishna reel extends the storybook register alongside the clay and 2D examples and sets the full-animation bar, the gold-diagram and cream-paper reels add two ways to make abstract ideas visible without characters, the Oppenheimer reel models a history-first hook, the wireframe reel adds a puppet-animation option with a late colour payoff, and the two painterly reels show a micro-format and a struggle-to-reveal structure for Hanuman material
